# CheckOff Destination Hub Philosophy

CheckOff inspires people to discover destinations, helps visitors explore more deeply once they arrive, and creates reasons to return again and again.

CheckOff creates value by:

- inspiring tourism
- capturing tourism
- deepening tourism
- encouraging repeat tourism
- connecting nearby Metro users to worthwhile destinations
- re-engaging prior visitors when seasons change
- recommending additional participating destinations based on actual CheckOff behavior

A CheckOff Destination Hub is not a static city guide or business directory.

It is a managed visitor-engagement system built around:

- curated experiences
- substantial themed lists
- seasonal rotation
- timely Spotlights
- physical activation
- Metro discovery
- visitor CheckOff history
- behavioral re-engagement
- optional Destination Partner initiatives

Every destination should be evaluated through this lens.

## Population is secondary. Visitor economy and management complexity are primary.

Resident population alone does NOT determine whether a destination is attractive or how much a Hub may ultimately cost.

A town of 4,000 residents with strong tourism, many experiences, meaningful seasonal change, and multiple feeder Metros may be a much stronger CheckOff opportunity than a town of 25,000 residents with little visitor activity.

Likewise, a tourism destination with only 12,000 residents but millions of annual visitors and hundreds of visitor-facing businesses may require a much larger and more expensive Hub than a small rural weekend destination.

# Current CheckOff Target Profile

For the first approximately 20–25 Destination Hubs, CheckOff should generally prioritize:

- smaller U.S. leisure destinations
- places people intentionally travel TO
- strong weekend / road-trip identity
- meaningful visitor draw
- substantial CheckOffable experience inventory
- one or more feeder Metros within a realistic drive radius
- strong seasonal or repeat-visit potential
- a likely Chamber, DMO, City, tourism office, or similar Champion
- reasonable physical activation opportunities
- potential to strengthen the larger CheckOff Destination Network

The strongest early targets will often be approximately **1–3 hours from a meaningful Metro**, but this is not a rigid cutoff.

A more distant destination may still score strongly as a:

- Weekend Getaway
- Road Trip
- Seasonal Escape
- Vacation destination

Destinations with little outside visitor draw, very limited experience inventory, no meaningful feeder market, or little reason for repeat visits may still become relevant to CheckOff later, but should generally not be prioritized today.

# Core Commercial Principle

The primary Destination buyer is the **Destination Champion**.

Typical Champions include:

- Chamber of Commerce
- Destination Marketing Organization
- Convention & Visitors Bureau
- City tourism office
- Economic development organization
- Other destination-wide tourism organization

The Champion funds **breadth across the destination**.

Additional organizations may later become **Destination Partners** when they add meaningful separately managed scope.

Examples:

- wine association
- lodging group
- downtown organization
- City department
- outdoor/birding organization
- agritourism coalition
- arts organization
- major event organization

Destination Partner potential matters because it increases the long-term commercial value of a Hub.

Do NOT assume that every possible partner would sign.

DVA-1 only identifies whether that ecosystem appears to exist.

## Scoring Definitions

### Visitor Appeal

Evaluate whether people already have a reason to travel to this destination.

Signals may include:

- established tourism identity
- signature attractions
- tourism traffic
- lodging economy
- well-known events
- scenic or recreational draw
- food / wine / cultural reputation

---

### CheckOff Opportunity / Discovery Gap

Evaluate whether CheckOff could help visitors discover more than the destination's obvious anchor attraction.

Strong signals:

- visitors arrive for one reason but miss many others
- tourism activity concentrates in one corridor or attraction
- local experiences are poorly surfaced
- hidden gems exist
- businesses close early because visitors leave
- visitors have difficulty understanding what else to do

---

### Experience Inventory Potential

Estimate the likely pool of strong, specific, CheckOffable experiences.

Do NOT conduct a full inventory.

Use high-level evidence from tourism pages, maps, visitor guides, and obvious business density.

General guidance:

- ⭐ Very limited: fewer than roughly 20 plausible experiences
- ⭐⭐ Approximately 20–30
- ⭐⭐⭐ Approximately 30–50
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Approximately 50–80+
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Likely 80–150+ strong candidates or a very deep tourism ecosystem

These are screening ranges, not package limits.

A larger raw business count does not automatically earn a higher score. The question is how much **quality visitor-facing CheckOff inventory** appears to exist.

---

### Seasonal / Repeat Visit Potential

Evaluate whether the destination offers meaningfully different reasons to return.

Consider:

- four-season changes
- harvests
- snow
- water recreation
- bird migration
- wine releases
- festivals
- holiday programming
- outdoor seasons
- recurring events
- shoulder-season opportunities

A destination with different spring, summer, fall, and winter experiences is especially attractive because CheckOff can rotate lists and re-engage prior visitors.

---

### Drive Market Accessibility

Evaluate access to realistic feeder populations.

Trip-type guidance:

- **Day Trip:** approximately 0–2.5 hours
- **Weekend Getaway:** approximately 1.5–4 hours
- **Road Trip / Seasonal Escape:** approximately 2.5–5+ hours
- **Vacation:** may exceed these ranges if the draw is sufficiently strong

Do NOT penalize a destination for being outside day-trip range if Weekend Getaway or Seasonal Escape is clearly the appropriate positioning.

---

### Visitor Leakage Opportunity

Evaluate whether CheckOff could plausibly help visitors:

- stay longer
- visit another business
- explore beyond the primary attraction
- add another activity
- return in another season
- stay overnight instead of leaving

This category is about **economic activity CheckOff could help retain or expand**.

---

### Destination Champion Potential

At DVA-1 depth, determine whether there appears to be a plausible organization capable of representing and funding the destination broadly.

Examples:

- Chamber
- DMO
- CVB
- City tourism office
- tourism authority
- economic development organization

Do NOT research specific contacts yet.

That belongs in DVA-2.

---

### Partner Depth

Evaluate whether the destination appears capable of supporting additional separately managed Destination Partner initiatives beyond the Champion.

Possible partner ecosystems:

- wine association
- lodging/hospitality group
- downtown district
- arts organization
- outdoor/birding coalition
- agritourism group
- City
- event organization
- recreation organization

Scoring guidance:

- ⭐ Little evidence of a partner ecosystem
- ⭐⭐ One possible additional organization
- ⭐⭐⭐ Several plausible partner categories
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Strong multi-organization tourism ecosystem
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Multiple obvious organizations or industries capable of supporting meaningful separate initiatives

Do NOT count individual businesses as Destination Partners.

Individual business upgrades are a separate CheckOff revenue layer.

---

### Activation Readiness

Evaluate whether the destination appears capable of physically putting CheckOff in front of visitors.

Signals include:

- hotels
- visitor centers
- attractions
- tasting rooms
- museums
- event venues
- downtown merchants
- tourism kiosks
- campgrounds
- vacation rentals
- tourism offices

High Activation Readiness means CheckOff would have many natural places for QR materials, visitor handouts, hotel cards, posters, rack cards, and similar assets.

---

### Regional Network Synergy

Evaluate how much this destination strengthens the existing and planned CheckOff Destination Network rather than standing alone.

Distance alone is not the deciding factor.

Score based on whether the destination fits at least one natural trip type and meaningfully strengthens a feeder Metro.

Consider:

- Is it within realistic range of an active CheckOff Metro?
- Is it within realistic range of a likely future Metro?
- Can the Metro naturally send Day Trip, Weekend Getaway, Road Trip, Seasonal Escape, or Vacation traffic here?
- Does this destination offer scenery, climate, elevation, water, recreation, culture, food, wine, or another experience the feeder Metro does not?
- Could users acquired in this destination later become valuable Metro users?
- Could users acquired through another Hub plausibly be cross-promoted here?
- Would this Hub fill a geographic, seasonal, or experience-category gap in CheckOff's network?

Scoring guidance:

- ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Significantly strengthens one or more Metro ecosystems and creates strong cross-Destination opportunities.
- ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Strong regional connection with obvious feeder-market potential.
- ⭐⭐⭐ Moderate regional value.
- ⭐⭐ Limited connection.
- ⭐ Largely standalone with little current CheckOff network value.

Keep this a high-level screening judgment.

## Important Pricing Rule

These ranges are internal screening estimates only.

Final pricing must consider:

1. Visitor economy
2. Curatable experience inventory
3. Geographic complexity
4. Seasonal/program complexity
5. Partner ecosystem
6. Feeder-Metro / network value

Population, land size, and raw business count are contextual inputs, not standalone pricing formulas.

A 12,000-person destination receiving millions of annual visitors may require a much larger Hub than a 25,000-person town with modest tourism.

# Agent Rules

- Be analytical, not descriptive.
- Think like a destination strategist, not a travel writer.
- Explain why each finding matters to CheckOff.
- Do not invent facts.
- Make reasonable estimates when necessary and label them as estimates.
- Population is context, not the core viability metric.
- Visitor activity and visitor-economy potential matter more than resident population.
- Do not confuse a **Destination Hub** with a **Metro**.
- A Destination is a place people intentionally travel TO.
- A Metro is primarily a feeder/home market where users live, discover local experiences, and discover nearby Destinations.
- A larger Destination does not automatically become a Metro because its population grows.
- Do not use fixed item-count packages such as 25-item or 40-item tiers as the basis of analysis.
- Experience counts are screening estimates used to understand management complexity, not contractual package limits.
- Do not assume every business belongs on a curated list.
- Do not assume every possible Destination Partner will pay.
- Do not perform DVA-2 research inside DVA-1.
- Keep the report concise.
- Prioritize decision-making over completeness.
- The purpose of DVA-1 is to qualify destinations, estimate likely scale, and decide whether deeper research is justified.
