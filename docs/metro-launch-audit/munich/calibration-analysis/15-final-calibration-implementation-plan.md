# Final Calibration Implementation Plan — Part 4 (What Winston Failed to Do) + Synthesis

This file carries Part 4 of the task (comparing Winston's original list output against the final rebuilt lists, mapped to pipeline stages) and closes out the extension with a single prioritized implementation plan spanning both the original 8-file analysis and this 7-file extension.

## Part 4 — What Winston failed to do, mapped to stages

### The headline fact from Part 2: Winston's list output was not lightly edited, it was replaced

`10-original-vs-final-list-decisions.md` establishes that only 2 of Winston's 62 original list-membership rows (across Fall 2026, After Dark, Cafés/Markets, Hidden Gems) survived by venue identity into the final version — and both survived only because the underlying *item* had already been rewritten during the original-70 cleanup pass, not because the list-curation step chose to keep something unchanged. This reframes every finding below: this isn't "Winston's lists needed touch-ups," it's "Winston's list-curation output was discarded wholesale and a different, external process built new lists from a much larger catalog." That's a legitimate, even necessary outcome given how much the catalog itself grew (70 → 184 items) — but it means the *list-curation* failure and the *catalog* failure are the same root cause, not two independent problems.

### Failure-by-failure mapping

**Weak original inclusions.** Documented exhaustively in `01-original-70-disposition.md`'s 14 probable-retirement table and reused directly in `10-original-vs-final-list-decisions.md`: generic-action items (Goldmarie, Café Erika, Café Maria — "savor/indulge/settle in," no specific order), a combined-business item (Beirut Beirut and Backsteinchen), a whole-district-as-venue item (Kunstareal Munich, Glockenbachviertel), a time-bound/expiring claim (Wiener Platz's "renovations... through 2027"). **Stage responsible:** M6_QUALITY_VERIFICATION should have caught the genericness and combined-business patterns before certification; M6.5's editorial pass is where "savor a dish" should have become a specific order. **Does the pipeline catch this now?** Yes for genericness (`checkDistinctiveExperience` via M5_75's `evaluateSeedCandidate`, Failure 7 in `05-pipeline-failure-stage-mapping.md`); not yet for combined-business (Failure 6, same file — no dedicated check exists in the read code).

**Strong experiences Winston failed to include.** Nearly the entire final catalog's list-worthy material (Gaststätte Großmarkthalle's before-noon Weißwurst ritual, Café Frischhut's dawn Auszogne, the beer-garden ritual language throughout Beer Gardens) came from the external Bulk Add process, not from Winston's original research. **Stage responsible:** M3_BROAD_DISCOVERY / M5_TARGETED_DEEP_DIVES — Winston's original discovery pass simply didn't surface this material. **Why not:** `04-bulk-add-decision-analysis.md` already found the Bulk Add process's source diversity (municipal tourism-board neighborhood guides, Eater, Atlas Obscura) meaningfully exceeded whatever Winston's original discovery pass drew from — this reads as a discovery-breadth gap at M3, not a list-curation gap at all.

**Category imbalance.** Winston's Fall 30 and After Dark both leaned on whatever categories the original 70 happened to contain (heavy Arts & Culture, thin Sports/Shopping/Social — `03-category-geography-comparison.md`). The final lists show real category discipline instead (Munich After Dark 75% Bar & drinks / 25% Nightlife, near-zero drift; Cafés/Markets 95% Food & drink). **Stage responsible:** M2_CATEGORY_COVERAGE_PLAN (setting targets the list-curation step could check against) and, per this extension's Part 6 finding, the list-curation step itself (M9) needs its own category-shape awareness, not just the catalog-level `categoryPolicy.ts` gate — a catalog can be well-balanced while an individual *list* is not (this didn't happen in Munich's final lists, but nothing in the read `buildHomeListPlan` code confirms a list-level category check exists).

**Geographic imbalance.** Winston's original lists (built entirely from the original-10-neighborhood catalog) had zero surrounding-municipality representation by construction. The final lists — especially Beer Gardens (9/20 items in surrounding municipalities) and Day Trips (all 20 items outside central Munich) — are where the wave2 geographic expansion actually pays off editorially. **Stage responsible:** M1_GEOGRAPHY_MAP (the boundary decision itself, already covered in `05-pipeline-failure-stage-mapping.md`'s Failure 1) feeding into M9's list construction. This is the clearest case in the whole analysis of an M1-level decision (what's in the metro) directly gating list quality three stages later — Day Trips & Big Adventures literally could not have existed under Winston's original M0/M1 boundary.

**Generic-or-famous-places incorrectly treated as hidden.** Winston's original Hidden Gems (9 items: Bei Dagmar, Viktualienmarkt, Zephyr Bar, etc.) had **zero items with any discovery mechanic** — "sample local specialties at Viktualienmarkt" and "sip a story-driven cocktail at Zephyr Bar" are not hidden-gem claims by any reading (`10-original-vs-final-list-decisions.md`). The final Hidden Gems list is a real improvement (2 of 24 items have a strong discovery mechanic, several more have a defensible one — `11-list-portfolio-scorecards.md`) but still has the same failure mode in miniature: ~58% of its 24 members have no discovery mechanic at all. **Stage responsible:** this is a list-curation-time judgment (M9, or whatever human/process built these six lists) — no earlier stage produces a "discovery basis" field to check against, which is exactly the gap `12-m9-training-requirements.md` proposes closing with a required `listSpecificEvidence` field.

**Items included only because they existed in the catalog.** Winston's original lists show this pattern directly — Boazn (generic), Café Ruffini, and Elisabethmarkt each appear on 2-3 of Winston's four lists with no list-specific rationale beyond "this is a reasonable Munich item and the list needed more members" (inferable from the fact that Winston's After Dark included Café Ruffini, whose only nighttime-relevant clause is "open until midnight" tacked onto an otherwise all-day café description). **Does the final version repeat this?** No — `11-list-portfolio-scorecards.md`'s per-list weak-filler review found no true filler in any of the six final lists, though Munich After Dark's 4 "not structurally nighttime" bar inclusions (Frisches Bier, Paulaner Bräuhaus, Higgins Ale Works, Zero Dosage) are the closest remaining trace of this pattern — legitimate bar experiences, softly justified as *nighttime-specific*.

**Missing independent-business representation.** Neither Winston's original catalog nor the Bulk Add process ever captured ownership as structured data (`00-executive-summary.md`, `02-new-item-contributions.md`) — this is unchanged by the list rebuild and remains the single highest-leverage gap identified across the whole calibration analysis (both the original 8 files and this extension). **Stage responsible:** M5_TARGETED_DEEP_DIVES populating a real `research_verifier` evidence contract — unimplemented, as established repeatedly.

**Missing nighttime logic.** Winston's original After Dark (10 items) had at least 3 members with no real nighttime-specific claim (Café Ruffini, Flex bar's foosball-and-music framing works at 2pm as well as 10pm, M'Uniqo's "360° views" isn't inherently a night claim). The final Munich After Dark is materially better (18/24 clearly bar-format, with the nighttime-suitability caveat above) but not perfect. **Stage responsible:** M6.5 (editorial writing should bake in a time-of-day hook when the list's promise requires one) or, per this extension's Part 6, an explicit M9 field (`nighttimeSpecific: boolean` with a reason) rather than relying on category match alone.

**Missing experience diversity.** Winston's Fall 30 had almost no Play/Sports/Adventure representation (the original 70's category skew, `03-category-geography-comparison.md`). The final Fall 2026 list includes Play (KartPalast, Bavaria Filmstadt), Sports (SAP Garden), Spa & self-care (Therme Erding, Munich Hot Springs) alongside its Food & drink core — genuinely more varied. **Stage responsible:** M2/M4 category-target setting feeding into whatever built the final Fall list.

**Failure to distinguish inventory quality from list quality.** This is the important structural point this whole extension surfaces: Winston's pipeline (per `05-pipeline-failure-stage-mapping.md`'s Failure 11) already learned this lesson at the *catalog* level — M5_75_SEED_PORTFOLIO_AUDIT exists specifically to validate portfolio shape before expensive per-item editorial work. **Nothing equivalent exists yet at the *list* level** — a themed list can be built from a catalog that already passed M5_75's portfolio audit and still be a weak list (as Winston's original four lists were, despite drawing from an eventually-fine catalog). `12-m9-training-requirements.md` proposes the fix: an M9-level `ItemListFitDecision` record and required `listSpecificEvidence`, mirroring M5_75's philosophy one stage later.

**Any list lacking a clear editorial promise.** All four of Winston's original lists had a stated title but no membership discipline enforcing it (see above, per list). All six final lists have a materially clearer promise-to-membership match (`11-list-portfolio-scorecards.md`), with Fall 2026's season-in-name-only issue as the one remaining case of title/membership disagreement.

### Why Winston never proposed Beer Gardens or Day Trips (task's specific ask)

Both lists are unambiguously strong, coherent, well-populated Munich-specific concepts — 20 items each, tight thematic identity, real geographic reach. Winston never proposed either. Three independent, compounding reasons, none of which is "Winston made a bad editorial call":

1. **The underlying catalog material didn't exist yet.** Winston's original 70-item catalog had almost no surrounding-municipality items and comparatively thin beer-garden-ritual-specific writing (its beer-garden content was mostly the two malformed/generic Hirschgarten and Augustiner-Keller originals). A themed list needs a critical mass of real, specific items to draw from — `THEMED_LIST_MIN_ITEMS = 8` in the real `metroLaunchDriver.ts` code is the concrete expression of this requirement. Neither cluster had 8 qualifying items until the Bulk Add process added the outer-municipality brewery/beer-garden and day-trip-attraction items.
2. **`THEMED_LIST_DEFINITIONS` is a static, predefined list, not a discovery mechanism.** Per `12-m9-training-requirements.md`'s read of `buildHomeListPlan`, `buildEditorialThemedLists` only finds members for a theme that's already been defined (by category/tag/pattern) ahead of time. Even with the right catalog material present, a "Beer Gardens" or "Day Trips" theme could only be surfaced by M9 if someone had already added a matching entry to `THEMED_LIST_DEFINITIONS` before the run. Winston's pipeline, as read, has no step that looks at a fully certified catalog and asks "is there a natural cluster here nobody defined a list for?" — that judgment call was made by a human (Jerry, external to this SQL-generation and driver-code path) when the final six lists were built.
3. **Geography and theme are coupled in a way the pipeline doesn't yet model.** Day Trips specifically only makes sense once "surrounding metro municipality" is an accepted, named tier of the catalog (m0.json's own boundary contradiction, `03-category-geography-comparison.md`) — a themed list can't be proposed around geography the metro-definition artifact still calls out-of-scope.

**Mapped to stages:** (1) is an M3/M5 discovery-breadth gap, already addressed by the time wave2 ran. (2) is the concrete M9 gap `12-m9-training-requirements.md` proposes a cluster-discovery pass for. (3) is the M0/M1 boundary-reconciliation gap `05-pipeline-failure-stage-mapping.md` and `03-category-geography-comparison.md` already flagged, now shown to have a second, list-curation-level consequence beyond the catalog-level one already documented.
