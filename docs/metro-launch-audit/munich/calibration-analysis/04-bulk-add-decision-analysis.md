# Bulk Add Decision Analysis (Deliverable F) — Part 4

## What artifacts actually reveal about candidate decisions

Only two of the five candidate-facing artifacts carry a decision signal beyond "accepted": wave1's CSV has `priority`/`score`/`source_keys`/`source_urls` columns (a scoring rubric applied to *accepted* candidates only — no rejected/held candidates are visible anywhere in any artifact), and `munich_bulk_expansion_wave2_corrected.sql` shows the actual insertion template's own internal guardrails. **No artifact anywhere records a REJECT, HOLD, or "not processed" decision with a reason.** Every CSV is a list of what was accepted; the universe of what was researched-and-declined is invisible to this analysis. This is disclosed as a hard limit, not glossed over — Part 4's classification below is therefore built from *indirect* evidence (what got in vs. what plausibly should have been considered and isn't there) rather than direct decision logs.

## Recurring reasons candidates were (evidently) accepted

Reading across all 4 CSVs' `checkoff_item`/`body` columns for what got *in*, the Bulk Add Agent consistently favored: a single named venue (never a whole neighborhood or park as the "venue," with the exception of the wave2 lake/hiking entries which name a specific trail or beach), a concrete action verb tied to something specific to that venue (a named dish, a named drink, a specific physical detail — "the ceiling-high collection," "the rock-bar jukebox," "the tallest route you can finish"), and — per the task brief's own read of the CSVs — a heavy skew toward "local love" neighborhood-tip pages and Eater's Munich guide as sourcing, both of which are curated "worth telling a visitor about" lists rather than raw business directories. This is a meaningfully higher bar than Winston's original body style (compare "Savor an Alpine cuisine dish at 'Goldmarie'" to "Watch an Auszogne hit the fryer at dawn at 'Café Frischhut'") — the Bulk Add Agent's *editorial* judgment about what makes a compelling CheckOff action was, on the accepted evidence, considerably stronger than Winston's own M6.5-equivalent output on the original 70.

## Separating the six functions the task brief asks to distinguish

| Function | Evidence the Bulk Add process performed it well | Evidence it performed it poorly / not at all |
|---|---|---|
| **Research-discovery quality** | Wide source diversity (munich.travel neighborhood guides, Eater, Atlas Obscura, official venue sites, muenchen.de) across 4 waves; genuinely surfaced real, specific, checkable venues | 18% of wave1 (13/72) had no retrievable source at all ("verify during intake") |
| **CheckOff-worthiness judgment** | Body style overwhelmingly avoids the "visit/see/explore" genericness that plagued Winston's original 70 (see `03-category-geography-comparison.md`'s generic-action heuristic) | The Giesinger Grünspitz "Mitbringdinner on September 20, 2026" item bakes in a hard-coded, soon-to-expire date — not durably CheckOff-worthy as written |
| **Editorial CheckOffization** (turning a fact into a specific action) | Strong — "Rinse your own beer mug at the counter before getting it filled," "Listen for the bell announcing a fresh wooden barrel" are genuinely good, ritual-specific CheckOff writing | n/a — this is the strongest of the six functions |
| **Production-schema compliance** | Wave2's SQL shows real preflight discipline: metro-row existence check, all-26-neighborhoods-exist check, all-tags-exist check, exact-body-duplicate check, 8-tags-per-item postflight count check, `RAISE EXCEPTION` on any mismatch, single `BEGIN...COMMIT` transaction | `difficulty` is hardcoded to the literal `1` in every single wave2 INSERT (not computed, not varied) and `is_secret`/`secret_reveal_text` are not in the wave2 INSERT's column list at all — **the SQL-generation template itself has no mechanism to populate either field**, which is a stronger finding than "the Agent chose not to": there was structurally no way for wave2 candidates to end up with `is_secret=true` or `difficulty≠1` regardless of what the source research found |
| **Duplicate prevention** | Exact-body-string guard present in wave2's SQL | Insufficient — same-venue/different-wording duplicates (Kunst Oase, Vereinsheim) were not caught, because they don't produce identical body strings |
| **Geographic assignment** | Every wave2 item is correctly, specifically addressed (`maps_query` carries a full street address, unlike wave1's area-name-only rows) and neighborhood-matched via an explicit existence check against the metro's real neighborhood table | The neighborhood *set itself* was expanded ad hoc across multiple manual batches with no single recorded boundary decision (see `03-category-geography-comparison.md`'s m0.json contradiction) |
| **Final catalog-selection judgment** | Category mix ended up materially healthier than Winston's original (see `03-category-geography-comparison.md`) | Achieved via volume (2.6x growth) rather than deliberate rebalancing/pruning of the overrepresented categories |

**Conclusion for Winston's pipeline:** these six functions do not naturally live in one stage. Research-discovery quality and CheckOff-worthiness judgment belong before/during M3-M5 (discovery and deep-dives); editorial CheckOffization is M6.5's job by design; production-schema compliance (the preflight/postflight discipline seen in wave2's SQL) is a good model for **any** stage that writes to `public.items`, not just one; duplicate prevention needs to happen at the seed stage (M5_75, via `seedDuplicateNormalization.ts`) **before** editorial writing, precisely because Munich's own process proved that a same-venue-different-wording duplicate survives an exact-string check; geographic assignment needs the boundary decision made once, explicitly, and enforced consistently rather than re-decided per batch; final catalog-selection judgment (what M5_75's `SeedPortfolioAuditReport` executive verdict is meant to produce) needs to weigh category/geography shape as its own gate, separate from "did enough individually-good items get written."
